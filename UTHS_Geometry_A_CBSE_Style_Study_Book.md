# Master Formula Sheet

## Coordinate Geometry

Distance between points \((x_1,y_1)\) and \((x_2,y_2)\):

\[
d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}
\]

Midpoint:

\[
M=\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)
\]

Slope:

\[
m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}
\]

Parallel lines:

\[
m_1=m_2
\]

Perpendicular lines:

\[
m_1m_2=-1
\]

Point-slope form:

\[
y-y_1=m(x-x_1)
\]

Slope-intercept form:

\[
y=mx+b
\]

## Triangles

Triangle angle sum:

\[
A+B+C=180^\circ
\]

Pythagorean theorem:

\[
a^2+b^2=c^2
\]

Triangle inequality:

\[
a+b>c,\quad b+c>a,\quad c+a>b
\]

Area of triangle:

\[
A=\frac{1}{2}bh
\]

## Similarity and Proportions

If two triangles are similar:

\[
\frac{\text{side 1 of triangle A}}{\text{matching side 1 of triangle B}}
=
\frac{\text{side 2 of triangle A}}{\text{matching side 2 of triangle B}}
\]

Dilation with scale factor \(k\):

\[
(x,y)\rightarrow(kx,ky)
\]

## Right Triangle Trigonometry

For an acute angle \(\theta\):

\[
\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}
\]

\[
\cos\theta=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}
\]

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}
\]

Memory device:

\[
\text{SOH-CAH-TOA}
\]

## Special Right Triangles

45-45-90:

\[
x,\ x,\ x\sqrt2
\]

30-60-90:

\[
x,\ x\sqrt3,\ 2x
\]

## Right Triangle Altitude to Hypotenuse

If altitude \(h\) is drawn to the hypotenuse and splits the hypotenuse into parts \(p\) and \(q\):

\[
h^2=pq
\]

Each leg is the geometric mean of the whole hypotenuse and its adjacent segment:

\[
\text{leg}^2=(\text{hypotenuse})(\text{adjacent hypotenuse segment})
\]

# Chapter 1: Plan and Solve Real-World Problems

## What You Must Learn

- Convert a word problem into a diagram or equation.
- Identify what is given and what must be found.
- Use geometry formulas in practical situations.
- Check whether the answer is reasonable.
- Explain the steps of your solution.

## CBSE-Style Problem-Solving Method

1. Read the problem twice.
2. Draw a clean diagram.
3. Write the given information.
4. Mark the unknown as \(x\).
5. Choose the correct theorem or formula.
6. Solve.
7. Check units and reasonableness.

## Useful Representations

| Representation | Use |
|---|---|
| Diagram | Geometry shapes, angle relations, similar triangles |
| Table | Pattern problems or comparing dimensions |
| Graph | Coordinate geometry and lines |
| Number line | Segment lengths |
| Venn diagram | Logic/classification problems |

## Diagram: Shelf Parallel to Floor

```text
        A
       / \
      /   \
     D-----E     DE parallel BC
    /       \
   B---------C
```

When a segment inside a triangle is parallel to one side, it creates similar triangles.

If \(DE \parallel BC\), then:

\[
\frac{AD}{AB}=\frac{AE}{AC}
\]

or

\[
\frac{BD}{BA}=\frac{CE}{CA}
\]

depending on which lengths are given.

## Solved Example

In triangle \(ABC\), \(AB=7\), \(AC=9\). A shelf \(DE\) is parallel to \(BC\). Point \(D\) is on \(AB\) and \(BD=4\). Find \(CE\).

Since \(AB=7\) and \(BD=4\):

\[
AD=7-4=3
\]

Because \(DE\parallel BC\), triangles \(ADE\) and \(ABC\) are similar.

\[
\frac{AD}{AB}=\frac{AE}{AC}
\]

\[
\frac{3}{7}=\frac{AE}{9}
\]

\[
AE=\frac{27}{7}=3.86
\]

So:

\[
CE=AC-AE=9-3.86=5.14
\]

Answer: \(5.14\) feet from \(C\).

## Practice Questions

1. A ladder leans against a wall. The ladder is 13 ft long and its base is 5 ft from the wall. How high does it reach?
2. A map scale says 1 inch represents 8 miles. If two towns are 3.5 inches apart on the map, what is the real distance?
3. A rectangular garden is twice as long as it is wide. Its perimeter is 60 m. Find its dimensions.
4. A 6 ft person casts a 4 ft shadow. A building casts a 30 ft shadow. How tall is the building?
5. A triangle has sides 7 cm, 10 cm, and \(x\) cm. Give the possible range of \(x\).

## Answers

1. 12 ft
2. 28 miles
3. Width 10 m, length 20 m
4. 45 ft
5. \(3<x<17\)

# Chapter 2: Coordinate Geometry

## What You Must Learn

- Find length of a segment on a number line and coordinate plane.
- Use distance formula, midpoint formula, and slope formula.
- Connect distance formula to the Pythagorean theorem.
- Decide whether lines are parallel, perpendicular, or neither.
- Write equations of lines through a point.
- Find perpendicular bisectors.

## Segment on Number Line

Distance between \(a\) and \(b\):

\[
|b-a|
\]

Example:

Distance between \(-4\) and \(9\):

\[
|9-(-4)|=13
\]

## Distance Formula Diagram

```text
(x1,y1) o
        |\
        | \
  Δy    |  \ distance
        |   \
        o----o (x2,y2)
          Δx
```

The distance formula comes from:

\[
(\Delta x)^2+(\Delta y)^2=d^2
\]

## Slope Meaning

\[
m=\frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}
\]

| Slope Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Positive | Line rises left to right |
| Negative | Line falls left to right |
| Zero | Horizontal line |
| Undefined | Vertical line |

## Perpendicular Bisector

A perpendicular bisector:

- passes through the midpoint of a segment
- has slope that is the negative reciprocal of the segment's slope

## Solved Example

Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of segment \(EF\), where \(E(1,2)\) and \(F(5,0)\).

Step 1: Find midpoint.

\[
M=\left(\frac{1+5}{2},\frac{2+0}{2}\right)=(3,1)
\]

Step 2: Find slope of \(EF\).

\[
m_{EF}=\frac{0-2}{5-1}=\frac{-2}{4}=-\frac12
\]

Step 3: Perpendicular slope is \(2\).

Step 4: Use point-slope form.

\[
y-1=2(x-3)
\]

\[
y=2x-5
\]

Answer: \(y=2x-5\).

## Practice Questions

1. Find the distance between \((2,3)\) and \((8,11)\).
2. Find the midpoint of \((-4,5)\) and \((6,-1)\).
3. Find the slope of the line through \((1,7)\) and \((5,-1)\).
4. Are lines with slopes \(\frac23\) and \(-\frac32\) perpendicular?
5. Write the equation of the line through \((2,-3)\) parallel to \(y=4x+1\).
6. Write the equation of the line through \((6,2)\) perpendicular to \(y=-3x+8\).

## Answers

1. 10
2. \((1,2)\)
3. \(-2\)
4. Yes
5. \(y=4x-11\)
6. \(y=\frac13x\)

# Chapter 3: Logical Argumentation

## What You Must Learn

- Undefined terms, definitions, postulates, conjectures, and theorems.
- Conditional, converse, inverse, and contrapositive.
- Biconditional statements.
- Counterexamples.
- Euclidean and spherical geometry differences.

## Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Undefined term | Basic term accepted without definition, such as point, line, plane |
| Definition | Exact meaning of a term |
| Postulate | Accepted as true without proof |
| Conjecture | Educated guess based on patterns |
| Theorem | Statement proven true |
| Counterexample | One example that proves a conjecture false |

## Conditional Statements

Original:

\[
\text{If }p\text{, then }q
\]

| Statement | Form |
|---|---|
| Conditional | If \(p\), then \(q\) |
| Converse | If \(q\), then \(p\) |
| Inverse | If not \(p\), then not \(q\) |
| Contrapositive | If not \(q\), then not \(p\) |

Important rule:

The conditional and contrapositive always have the same truth value.

The converse and inverse always have the same truth value.

## Solved Example

Statement: If something is a blip, then it is a blop.

Which must be true?

Only the contrapositive must be true:

If something is not a blop, then it is not a blip.

## Biconditional

A biconditional uses “if and only if.”

Example:

A polygon is a triangle if and only if it has exactly 3 sides.

This means both directions are true:

- If it is a triangle, then it has 3 sides.
- If it has 3 sides, then it is a triangle.

## Euclidean vs Spherical Geometry

| Idea | Euclidean Geometry | Spherical Geometry |
|---|---|---|
| Surface | Flat plane | Sphere |
| Parallel lines | Exist | Great circles eventually meet |
| Triangle angle sum | Exactly \(180^\circ\) | Greater than \(180^\circ\) |

On Earth-like spherical geometry, a triangle can have angle sum more than \(180^\circ\), such as \(185^\circ\).

## Practice Questions

1. Write the converse: If a figure is a square, then it is a rectangle.
2. Write the contrapositive: If two lines are perpendicular, then they intersect.
3. Is this true? If a number is divisible by 4, then it is even.
4. Give a counterexample: All rectangles are squares.
5. In Euclidean geometry, what is the sum of triangle angles?
6. In spherical geometry, can triangle angle sum be greater than \(180^\circ\)?

## Answers

1. If a figure is a rectangle, then it is a square.
2. If two lines do not intersect, then they are not perpendicular.
3. True
4. A \(2\times5\) rectangle
5. \(180^\circ\)
6. Yes

# Chapter 4: Geometric Patterns, Conjectures, and Constructions

## What You Must Learn

- Angles formed by parallel lines and transversals.
- Interior/exterior angles of polygons.
- Triangle inequality theorem.
- Basic compass-straightedge constructions.
- Conjectures about diagonals, polygons, triangles, and circles.

## Parallel Lines and Transversal

```text
l1:  ----------------------
              /
             /
l2:  -------/--------------
```

If two parallel lines are cut by a transversal:

| Angle Pair | Relationship |
|---|---|
| Corresponding angles | Congruent |
| Alternate interior angles | Congruent |
| Alternate exterior angles | Congruent |
| Same-side interior angles | Supplementary |

## Polygon Formulas

Interior angle sum of an \(n\)-gon:

\[
(n-2)180^\circ
\]

Each interior angle of a regular \(n\)-gon:

\[
\frac{(n-2)180^\circ}{n}
\]

Each exterior angle of a regular \(n\)-gon:

\[
\frac{360^\circ}{n}
\]

## Triangle Inequality

For side lengths \(a,b,c\), a triangle exists only if:

\[
a+b>c,\quad b+c>a,\quad c+a>b
\]

Shortcut:

The third side must be greater than the difference and less than the sum of the other two sides.

If sides are 7 and 10:

\[
10-7<x<10+7
\]

\[
3<x<17
\]

## Construction Ideas

| Construction | Key Idea |
|---|---|
| Congruent segment | Copy length using compass |
| Segment bisector | Draw equal arcs from endpoints |
| Perpendicular bisector | Arcs above and below segment from both endpoints |
| Angle bisector | Equal arcs from angle sides, then connect vertex to arc intersection |
| Parallel line through point | Copy corresponding angle |
| Perpendicular line | Use equal arcs to create right angle |

## Diagram: Angle Bisector Construction

```text

      /
     /  )
    /  )  equal arcs
   O-----------A
       \
        \  angle bisector
```

5. What construction uses arcs from both endpoints of a segment to locate a line through the middle?
6. Find each interior angle of a regular octagon.

## Answers

# Chapter 5: Proofs and Congruencies

## What You Must Learn

- Vertical angles, linear pairs, parallel-line angle relationships.
- Triangle congruence: SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, HL.
- CPCTC: corresponding parts of congruent triangles are congruent.
- Two-column proofs.
- Pythagorean theorem, isosceles triangle theorem, midsegments, medians.
- Prove/classify quadrilaterals.

## Congruent Triangles

Two triangles are congruent if they have the same size and same shape.

| Rule | Meaning |
|---|---|
| SSS | Three pairs of sides congruent |
| SAS | Two sides and included angle congruent |
| ASA | Two angles and included side congruent |
| AAS | Two angles and non-included side congruent |
| HL | Right triangles with hypotenuse and one leg congruent |

Not valid:

- AAA proves similarity, not congruence.
- SSA is not generally valid.

## CPCTC

After proving triangles congruent, you may say:

\[
\text{Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent}
\]

This is used to prove individual sides or angles equal.

## Common Proof Reasons

| Statement | Reason |
|---|---|
| Given information | Given |
| Vertical angles are equal | Vertical Angles Theorem |
| Angles on a straight line sum to \(180^\circ\) | Linear Pair Postulate |
| Alternate interior angles equal | Parallel lines cut by transversal |
| Shared side equal to itself | Reflexive Property |
| Triangles congruent | SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, or HL |
| Corresponding parts equal | CPCTC |

## Diagram: Vertical Angles

```text
    \ 1 /
     \ /
  4   X   2
     / \
    / 3 \
```

\[
\angle1\cong\angle3,\quad \angle2\cong\angle4
\]

## Quadrilateral Tests

| Figure | How to Prove |
|---|---|
| Parallelogram | Both pairs opposite sides parallel, or opposite sides congruent, or diagonals bisect each other |
| Rectangle | Parallelogram with one right angle, or diagonals congruent |
| Rhombus | Parallelogram with all sides congruent, or diagonals perpendicular |
| Square | Rectangle and rhombus together |

## Solved Example

Which property is least useful when proving two triangles congruent: vertical angles, ASA, AAS, or Pythagorean theorem?

Vertical angles can help prove an angle pair. ASA and AAS are congruence rules. The Pythagorean theorem finds side lengths in right triangles, but it is not usually a direct triangle congruence method.

Answer: Pythagorean theorem.

## Practice Questions

1. Which rule proves congruence if three pairs of sides are equal?
2. Which rule proves congruence in right triangles using hypotenuse and one leg?
3. If \(\triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF\), what side corresponds to \(AB\)?
4. Are AAA and SSA valid congruence rules?
5. If a quadrilateral has diagonals that bisect each other, what can you prove?
6. If a parallelogram has perpendicular diagonals, what special type may it be?

## Answers

1. SSS
2. HL
3. \(DE\)
4. No
5. It is a parallelogram
6. Rhombus

# Chapter 6: Dilations and Similar Triangles

## What You Must Learn

- A dilation changes size but preserves shape.
- Scale factor \(k\) multiplies side lengths.
- Angle measures stay the same under dilation.
- Similar triangles have equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides.
- AA similarity can prove triangles are similar.

## Dilation

If scale factor \(k=4\), every side length becomes 4 times larger.

Angles do not change.

```text
Small triangle: sides 3, 4, 5
Scale factor 4
Large triangle: sides 12, 16, 20
```

## Similar Triangle Notation

If:

\[
\triangle ABC\sim\triangle DEF
\]

then:

\[
\angle A\cong\angle D,\quad \angle B\cong\angle E,\quad \angle C\cong\angle F
\]

and:

\[
\frac{AB}{DE}=\frac{BC}{EF}=\frac{AC}{DF}
\]

## Diagram: Similar Triangles

```text
     A
    / \
   /   \
  B-----C

       D
      / \
     /   \
    /     \
   E-------F
```

## Solved Example

\(\triangle OLD\) is enlarged by scale factor 4 to become \(\triangle NEW\). If side \(NE\) corresponds to side \(OL=4\), then:

\[
NE=4(4)=16
\]

If \(\angle W\) corresponds to \(\angle D=72^\circ\), then:

\[
m\angle W=72^\circ
\]

Answer: \(NE=16,\ m\angle W=72^\circ\).

## Practice Questions

1. A triangle has sides 5, 6, 8. It is dilated by scale factor 3. What are the new sides?
2. If \(\triangle ABC\sim\triangle XYZ\), which angle corresponds to \(\angle B\)?
3. If two triangles have two pairs of equal angles, what similarity rule applies?
4. If a 4 cm side corresponds to a 10 cm side, what is the scale factor from small to large?
5. Two similar triangles have corresponding sides 6 and 18. If another side in the smaller triangle is 7, what is the matching side in the larger triangle?
6. Does dilation change angle measures?

## Answers

1. 15, 18, 24
2. \(\angle Y\)
3. AA similarity
4. \(2.5\)
5. 21
6. No

# Chapter 7: Similarity Theorems and Right-Triangle Altitude

## What You Must Learn

- Triangle proportionality theorem.
- Similarity proofs.
- Right triangle altitude to hypotenuse creates three similar triangles.
- Use geometric mean to solve altitude and leg problems.

## Triangle Proportionality Theorem

If a line parallel to one side of a triangle intersects the other two sides, it divides those sides proportionally.

```text
        A
       / \
      /   \
     D-----E    DE parallel BC
    /       \
   B---------C
```

\[
\frac{AD}{DB}=\frac{AE}{EC}
\]

Also:

\[
\triangle ADE\sim\triangle ABC
\]

## Right Triangle Altitude Theorem

```text
        A
       /|\
      / | \
     /  |h \
    /   |   \
   B----D----C
       p  q
```

If \(AD\) is altitude to hypotenuse \(BC\):

\[
h^2=pq
\]

\[
AB^2=BC\cdot BD
\]

\[
AC^2=BC\cdot DC
\]

## Solved Example

The altitude to the hypotenuse splits the hypotenuse into parts 9 and 16. Find the altitude.

\[
h^2=9\cdot16
\]

\[
h^2=144
\]

\[
h=12
\]

Answer: 12.

## Practice Questions

1. A line parallel to one side of a triangle creates a smaller triangle. Are the two triangles similar?
2. In a right triangle, the altitude to the hypotenuse splits it into 4 and 9. Find the altitude.
3. If \(h^2=25\cdot4\), find \(h\).
4. A right triangle hypotenuse is 20. One segment of the hypotenuse is 5. Find the leg adjacent to the 5 segment.
5. If \(\triangle ADE\sim\triangle ABC\), and \(AD=3\), \(AB=12\), \(AE=5\), find \(AC\).
6. What theorem says a parallel segment divides two sides proportionally?

## Answers

1. Yes
2. 6
3. 10
4. 10
5. 20
6. Triangle proportionality theorem

## Labeling a Right Triangle

For angle \(\theta\):

```text
        /|
       / |

     /θ  |
    /____|
   adjacent
```

## SOH-CAH-TOA

\[
\sin\theta=\frac{O}{H}
\]

\[
\cos\theta=\frac{A}{H}
\]

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{O}{A}
\]

## Choosing the Ratio

| Given/Need | Ratio |
|---|---|
| Opposite and hypotenuse | Sine |
| Adjacent and hypotenuse | Cosine |
| Opposite and adjacent | Tangent |

## Solved Example

In a right triangle, an angle is \(37^\circ\). The side adjacent to the angle is 11 m. Find the hypotenuse \(a\).

Use cosine:

\[
\cos37^\circ=\frac{11}{a}
\]

\[
a=\frac{11}{\cos37^\circ}
\]

\[
a\approx13.77
\]

Answer: \(13.77\) m.

# Exam Strategy

## For Multiple Choice Geometry

1. Draw or redraw every diagram.
2. Mark equal angles, equal sides, and parallel lines.
3. Estimate before calculating when possible.
4. Use answer choices to check reasonableness.
5. If stuck, eliminate impossible answers first.

## Common Mistakes To Avoid

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Using sine instead of cosine | Label opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse first |
| Forgetting angle measures do not change under dilation | Only side lengths scale |
| Treating AAA as congruence | AAA proves similarity only |
| Forgetting negative reciprocal for perpendicular slope | Multiply slopes to check \(-1\) |
| Using \(a+b\ge c\) for triangle inequality | Must be \(a+b>c\) |
| Mixing corresponding sides in proportions | Write similarity statement carefully |
